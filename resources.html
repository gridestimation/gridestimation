Project Document Checklist

What to send when requesting an estimate

Send all available documents. Missing information can be noted, clarified or handled with stated assumptions where appropriate.

Essential Project Information

  • Complete architectural, structural and MEP drawings
  • Project specifications and material requirements
  • Bid invitation or tender instructions
  • Project address and construction location
  • Bid closing date or required delivery date
  • Requested trade or estimating scope
  • Applicable addenda and drawing revisions

Helpful Additional Information

  • Bid forms and pricing schedules
  • Preferred labor rates or union requirements
  • Supplier quotations already received
  • Equipment rental or subcontractor quotations
  • General conditions and project duration
  • Required alternates, allowances and unit prices
  • Preferred estimate template or sample format
Helpful tip: Rename files clearly and send the latest drawing set together with all issued addenda. This helps reduce confusion between outdated and current documents.
Before You Submit

Construction estimate review checklist

Review these items before finalizing pricing and submitting your bid.

  • Confirm the drawing set: Make sure the estimate reflects the latest plans, revisions and addenda.
  • Verify scope: Check that the estimated work matches the scope you intend to include in your bid.
  • Review assumptions: Confirm that estimated allowances and assumptions are acceptable.
  • Check exclusions: Identify excluded work that may need to be added or clearly stated in the proposal.
  • Update quotations: Replace budget pricing with current supplier or subcontractor quotations when available.
  • Add project costs: Review taxes, permits, insurance, bonding, overhead, profit and escalation requirements.
  • Review the bid form: Transfer pricing carefully into the required submission format.

Estimating Terminology

Common construction estimating terms

Quantity Takeoff

Measurement and listing of materials, systems or work items shown in the project documents.

Unit Cost

Cost assigned to one unit of work, such as each, square foot, linear foot or cubic yard.

Allowance

A budget amount included for work or materials that are not fully defined at the time of estimating.

Alternate

Optional scope priced separately from the base bid.

General Conditions

Project-level costs such as supervision, temporary facilities, safety and site management.

Contingency

An amount included to address uncertainty or undefined project risks.

Markup

An amount or percentage added for overhead, profit, risk or other business costs.

Addendum

A formal change or clarification issued before the bid closing date.

Resource Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you prepare an estimate if some drawings are missing?

We can review the available documents and identify whether the scope can be estimated. Missing information may require assumptions or exclusions.

Should I send specifications with the drawings?

Yes. Specifications often contain material, installation, testing and manufacturer requirements that may not appear directly on the drawings.

Can you use our company’s estimate template?

You may provide your preferred template for review. Available quantities and pricing can be organized according to the agreed format.

Do I need supplier quotes before requesting an estimate?

Supplier quotes are helpful but not always required. They can be incorporated when available.

Can you help with a specific construction trade?

Yes. Grid Estimations supports complete project estimates and individual trade scopes.
